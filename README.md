# SML_AAT: AirWatch Bengaluru

Statistical Modeling (24AM5PCSML) AAT project. Aligned with **SDG 11.6** (air quality in cities) and **SDG 3.9** (health effects of pollution).

## What the project does
Using daily CPCB air-quality data for Bengaluru (Nov 2015 to Jul 2020), the project applies **Units 1-4** of the syllabus to two questions:
1. **Which pollutants and seasons are linked to fine particulate matter (PM2.5)?** Simple and multiple linear regression, assumption tests with remedies, influence diagnostics, and AIC/BIC/Cp.
2. **Can we flag unhealthy days (PM2.5 > 60 µg/m³)?** Contingency tables, chi-square tests, odds ratios and logistic regression with sensitivity, specificity and ROC.

**Key results:**
- PM10 alone explains 36% of daily PM2.5 variation. Adding the other pollutants and season raises this to 56%, and the log-transformed final model reaches 61% (adjusted R²; test R² 0.63).
- PM10, NOx and season matter most in the city-level model. The NO2 effect depends on the period.
- PM2.5 is about 30% lower in the monsoon than in winter, holding the other pollutants fixed.
- The odds of an unhealthy day are 4.4 times higher in winter (χ² = 111.4).
- The logistic model has a training AUC of 0.89 and catches 82% of unhealthy days at a 0.12 threshold.

The full summary and its limitations are in [`report/05a_phase1_results.md`](report/05a_phase1_results.md).

## Where each unit is covered
Everything is in one notebook: `notebooks/AirWatch_Bengaluru_SML_AAT.ipynb` (about 10 minutes to present).

| Unit | Syllabus topics | Notebook section | Report | Figure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Least squares, hypothesis tests and intervals, prediction, R² and adjusted R², ANOVA | §4 | `02_regression.md` | 02 |
| **2** | MLR, Gauss-Markov, residual analysis, VIF, heteroscedasticity, Durbin-Watson, remedies | §5-6 | `02_regression.md`, `03_diagnostics.md` | 03 |
| **2** | Ridge and LASSO | §9 | `06_feature_selection_regularisation.md` | 06 |
| **3** | Added-variable plots, studentized deleted residuals, hat matrix leverage, DFFITS, Cook's distance, DFBETAS | §7 | `03_diagnostics.md` | 04-05 |
| **3** | AIC, BIC, Mallow's Cp | §8 | `04_validation.md` | — |
| **4** | Contingency tables, odds ratio, logit, chi-square, logistic regression, inference, sensitivity and specificity, ROC | §10 | `05_logistic.md` | 07 |

Sections 1-3 cover the data, cleaning and EDA (report `01_problem_dataset_eda.md`, figure 01).

**Archive:** the original seven notebooks, including the beyond-syllabus extras (cross-validation, bootstrap, feature selection), are kept in `archive/original_notebooks/`, with their outputs in `archive/original_outputs/`.

## Folder layout
```
SML_AAT/
├── data/raw/          city_day.csv   (original CPCB data via Kaggle)
├── data/processed/    bengaluru_clean.csv   (created by the notebook)
├── notebooks/         AirWatch_Bengaluru_SML_AAT.ipynb, the full analysis
├── report/            written report sections (Markdown)
├── reports/figures/   all plots (created by the notebook)
├── reports/tables/    all result tables as CSV (created by the notebook)
├── archive/           original 01-07 notebooks and their outputs
├── final/             presentation (PowerPoint)
└── requirements.txt
```
Everything in `data/processed`, `reports/figures` and `reports/tables` is regenerated by running the notebook top to bottom.

## Setup and run (Windows, Python 3.13)
```
python -m venv venv
venv\Scripts\activate
pip install -r requirements.txt
```
Then open `notebooks/AirWatch_Bengaluru_SML_AAT.ipynb` in VS Code (kernel: `venv`) and choose **Run All**.
